### What this harness does and does not cover

`xtask/src/bench.rs` builds three byte-stable text-only PDFs **in memory**
(`small` 1 page, `medium` 100 pages, `large` 500 pages) and times five
structural proxies plus a peak-allocation estimate. It does not:

- run on `fixtures/realworld/**` (the committed corpus is never read);
- have an annotation/bookmark **edit** case (`OPERATION_NAMES` is `open`,
  `first_page_render`, `text_extract`, `search_index_build`, `save`);
- link `pdfxi-core`, `pdfxi-document`, `pdfxi-edit` or PDFium: its `open` and
  `save` are byte-scanning proxies, and its renderer is a mock;
- touch the filesystem at all, so its `save` (0.0142-3.2954 ms) excludes the
  durability flush that the shipping save performs.

It is therefore the wrong instrument for "open, edit and save timings on the
real corpus against explicit budgets". It still passes its own budgets, which is
recorded above as required.

### Budgets used

Two explicit budgets are reported for every case, and nothing was weakened:

1. **Derived budget** - the documented harness rule, applied literally to the
   fixture's page count: `max(base * pages / 500, 5 ms) * 1.25`, where `base` is
   1500 ms for `open` and 2000 ms for the incremental annotation-save row. The
   edit case has no row of its own, so the incremental-save base (2000 ms) is
   applied to it unchanged. For 1-3 page fixtures this yields 6.25-15.0 ms.
2. **Absolute budget** - the literal `docs/TESTING.md` ceiling for the row:
   `open` 1500 ms, `incremental annotation save` 2000 ms.

The derived budget is the stricter of the two everywhere, so it decides the
verdict.

## 3. Regression call

**Open and edit pass everywhere, with three orders of magnitude of headroom.**
Worst tracked medians: open 0.6685 ms (`powerpoint-slides.pdf`) and edit
0.5661 ms (`powerpoint-slides.pdf`), against derived budgets of 11.25 ms and
15.0 ms and absolute budgets of 1500 ms and 2000 ms.

**Two save cases are over the derived budget. This is stated plainly, not
rounded away:**

| Case | Tracked median (5 rounds) | Derived budget | Ratio | Absolute budget | Share of absolute |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `browser-print.pdf` save (1 page, 51,696 B) | 15.1668 ms | 6.25 ms | 2.43x over | 2000 ms | 0.76% |
| `microsoft-excel-2019-sample.pdf` save (2 pages, 92,366 B) | 10.4397 ms | 10.0 ms | 1.04x over | 2000 ms | 0.52% |

Both are recorded in `orchestration/blockers/T-0260B-budget.md` with the case,
number, budget and likely cause. No budget was changed and no case was dropped.

Persistence across every observation (all runs use the same code path):

| Run | `browser-print` save | `excel` save | `forms` save | `slides` save | Breaches |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| calibration A1 | 12.5064 | 7.9944 | 2.1947 | 9.3460 | 1 |
| calibration A2 (concurrent build) | 16.9441 | 12.8619 | 7.4282 | 21.6851 | 4 |
| tracked rounds 1-5 (table above) | 15.17-16.45 | 10.44-11.84 | 3.46-4.13 | 9.19-12.64 | 2 per round |
| final confirmation (after the script's own build) | 27.7821 | 17.8490 | 7.3643 | 18.7836 | 4 |

`browser-print.pdf` save breached the 6.25 ms derived budget in **all 11
observed runs** (12.06-27.78 ms), so that breach is real and load-independent in
direction. `excel-sample.pdf` save oscillates around its 10.0 ms derived budget
(7.99-17.85 ms): it breaches in the five tracked rounds and in loaded runs, and
passed in two lighter calibration rounds. `forms` and `slides` pass in every
quiescent round and breach only under concurrent build load. This is the
signature of filesystem/load latency, not of document-dependent work.

Likely cause (evidence, not guesswork):

- The shipping save is `crates/pdfxi-document/src/atomic.rs`: write a sibling
  temp file, `sync_all` it to the device, then rename. That is a durability
  flush, whose latency is dominated by the filesystem and its filter drivers,
  not by the byte count.
- The four save medians are **not** ordered by size: `browser-print.pdf`
  (51,696 B) 15.17-16.45 ms is slower than `powerpoint-slides.pdf`
  (119,341 B) 9.19-12.64 ms, and `pdfxi-forms-selfmade.pdf` (1,195 B) is
  3.46-4.13 ms. Size is not the driver.
- The harness `save` (which never touches the filesystem) is 0.0142-3.2954 ms
  on the same host, so the delta is file durability, not the writer.
- The 5 ms floor in the derived rule was calibrated on those in-memory
  fixtures. On this host a 1-page durability flush ranged 1.86-27.78 ms across
  observed runs, which straddles the 6.25 ms floor by up to 4.4x. The two
  breaches per tracked round are a budget-calibration artifact, not a
  user-visible slowdown: both cases use under 1% of the documented absolute
  budget.

Recommendation for the owner of `docs/TESTING.md` (not changed by this lane):
either give real-file save a disk-flush-aware floor (for example 25 ms), or
measure save without `sync_all` and report durability separately. Until then the
breach stands on record.

## 4. Limits

- Release profile, single process, warm page cache, one host (`HOME_PC`). The
  numbers are host-specific, exactly like the committed harness baseline.
- The corpus is small and text-heavy (1-3 pages, 1-119 KiB). It does not
  represent a 50 MB or 500 MB document; the 500-page reference row is covered
  separately by section 2.1 and the harness `large` fixture.
- Save numbers include NTFS durability (`sync_all`) and are the noisiest metric
  on this shared host: the same four saves ranged 1.86-27.78 ms across runs, and
  a run taken while another lane compiled doubled the open and edit medians too.
  That is why five rounds were recorded instead of one and why the verdict uses
  the lowest median; the per-run inventory is in section 3.
- The "edit" budget is the incremental-annotation-save row applied unchanged,
  because `docs/TESTING.md` has no separate edit row. The edit timed region
  includes the document clone and the annotation, so it is not understated.
- No render case: `first_page_render` remains the harness mock plus the
  `bench_open_500` first-page access; PDFium render timing is out of scope here.
- Encryption: open and edit of `pdfxi-encrypted-selfmade.pdf` are measured and
  pass; saving it is refused by design (`EncryptedSaveUnsupported`, ADR 0010
  point 5), so it is `unsupported`, not a pass.
- Verification is done through the shipping parser (reopen + annotation count).
  No independent PDF oracle was used.
